# The Picard height argument

The conditional theorem constructs a polynomial defining the ordinary integers from stated results about Prym varieties, isogenies, reduction, heights and elliptic-curve rank. The proof specializes these statements to the actual surface, including at the exceptional parameters. [Final theorem](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardConclusion.lean) · [Hypotheses and sources](../docs/qualitative-existence.md) · [Verified declarations](verification.json)

| Part of the proof | What Lean proves |
| --- | --- |
| Local arithmetic | One squarefree twist works simultaneously at every prime $`p\gt 3`$ where the primitive branch form has even valuation. The six roots split over a finite unramified extension. Their distances, clusters and principal-cluster parities satisfy the numerical conditions in the good-reduction criterion. Applying the criterion to the smooth proper completion proves good reduction of the actual local Jacobian. [Application](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardGoodReduction.lean) · [Global twist](PrimitiveTwist.lean) · [Local proof](LocalSupport.lean) |
| Invariant and height | The ordinary discriminant and the matching formula for $`I_2`$ give the invariant in the theta normalization. For period coordinates of this fibre Lean proves $`h(R)\le120h_\Theta`$. An isogeny of degree at most $`D`$ and Pazuki’s height comparison give $`h(R)\le84\max(1,h_F(B))+42\log D+289200`$. [Discriminant](PicardDiscriminant.lean) · [Theta comparison](ThetaComparison.lean) |
| Conjugation | Every Galois conjugate of the actual Jacobian over $`\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2},\sqrt{-3})`$ is isomorphic to it as a commutative group scheme. [Jacobian and base change](../geometry/README.md) |
| Descent | Laga and Shnidman’s explicit Prym equation specializes to the sextic. For two embeddings of a division quaternion algebra, one unit intertwines the entire pair of actions. At a reference fibre, a bijection on endomorphisms extends to rectangular matrices and lifts both maps splitting an idempotent. [Parameters and splitting](PrymFactor.lean) |
| Common field for descent | For a finite group and an algebraically closed coefficient field, Lean proves the required finiteness of second cohomology. Tate’s vanishing for discrete coefficients with trivial action then yields one finite Galois extension splitting every cocycle inflated from the given group. [Cohomological proof](UniformDescent.lean) · [Ribet, Theorem 6.3](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/korea.pdf) |
| Coefficient field and reduction | The splitting map spans a number field. If its cocycle comes from a quotient with exponent-two kernel, the degree is at most the order of that quotient. The image of the rational projector has the same dimension. Serre–Tate’s quotient and unramified descent corollaries transfer good reduction along a surjection from a positive power of the local Jacobian. [Dimension and projector](FibrewiseProjector.lean) · [Product models and quotient](AbelianPowers.lean) |

### How the hypotheses enter

**Descent.** Every nonzero twist is a literal instance of Laga and Shnidman’s sextic after a change of coefficients. The endomorphism parameters have sixth roots over one field, and the finite CM exceptions are pulled back along the actual marked parameter. Lean constructs compatible isogenies between conjugates, applies Guitart’s general formula to their cocycle, and descends the resulting isogeny to one finite normal field. [Twisted Prym equation](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardPrym.lean) · [Guitart’s inputs](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/GuitartSurfaces.lean) · [Uniform isogenies](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardConclusion.lean)

**Reduction and heights.** The common field embeds in a finite unramified local field containing all six roots away from a fixed set of primes. The reduction criterion and Serre–Tate transfer good reduction to the rational variety. Gaudron–Rémond bounds an isogeny in the opposite direction. The genus-two period comparison then applies to every smooth sextic via the rational point $`[R:1]`$. The level-two comparison and the stable height refer to the same Jacobian. [Local field](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/CommonWittField.lean) · [Stable heights](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/IsogenyHeight.lean) · [Period statement](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardPeriods.lean) · [Written argument](fibrewise-descent.md)

The half-logarithm bound in `IsogenyHeight.StableHeight` is von Känel’s equation (3.1) after semistable base change. The reference to Lemma 3.2 in the archived source comment points to a different bound in the same section. [von Känel, §3](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2021.29)

## Building the arithmetic and height proofs

From this directory run `lake exe cache get` and `lake build`. The build uses the adjacent bounded package’s twenty source modules, `ThetaQuotient`, `AbelianModel`, the four scheme-descent modules, and the application modules listed in the Lake target. Run `magma PicardFamily.m`, `magma PrymTwist.m` and `magma FibrewiseDescent.m` to check the sextic identities, the twist and the explicit field. The separate geometry package provides the projective curve and Jacobian proof. [Lake targets](lakefile.toml) · [Projective geometry build](../geometry/README.md)
