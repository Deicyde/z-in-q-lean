# A six-point height estimate sufficient to define the integers

The question here is whether one integer polynomial has rational projection exactly $`\mathbb Z`$. Its coefficients, degree and number of variables may be chosen in the proof. Lean proves Proposition 1 below for any uniform height constant. The final conditional theorem obtains the height estimate for the Picard family from named geometric, analytic and arithmetic hypotheses. The common field, rational abelian variety, local reductions and moduli-height estimate are deductions. The hypotheses that combine results from several papers are detailed below. Glazer’s bounded polynomial has its own theorem with a prescribed constant. ## The arithmetic reduction

Let $`\mathcal C=\{0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60\}`$ and let $`S`$ be the fixed finite set of primes used in Glazer’s multiplication construction. For $`t\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C`$, write $`M(t)`$ for the product of the primes $`p\notin S`$ for which $`v_p(t-c)`$ is positive and odd for some $`c\in\mathcal C`$. For a reduced fraction $`t=a/b`$ with $`b\gt 0`$, put $`h(t)=\log\max(|a|,b)`$.

**Proposition 1.** Assume the following two assertions.

1. There is a real constant $`C\gt 0`$, independent of $`t`$, such that $`h(t)\le C M(t)^{24}`$ for every $`t\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C`$.
2. For each positive squarefree $`d\equiv5\pmod 8`$ with odd second genus sum, the curve $`y^2=x^3-d^2x`$ has a rational point of infinite order.

Then $`\mathbb Z`$ is Diophantine over $`\mathbb Q`$. The second genus sum runs over unordered factorizations of $`d`$ into factors greater than one, one congruent to $`7`$, one to $`3`$, and the others to $`1`$ modulo $`8`$. The weight of a factorization is the product of $`|2\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-e}))|`$ over its factors $`e`$. Ye Tian’s Theorem 4.7 implies that an odd sum makes the normalized analytic value an odd integer. Tian assigns this value zero at order of vanishing at least two. The functional equation has sign $`-1`$, so the central zero is simple. The rank theorem for rational elliptic curves then gives a point of infinite order. The change $`(x,y)\mapsto(dx,d^2y)`$ takes the congruent-number curve $`dy^2=x^3-x`$ to the model above. Lean identifies the project's coefficient with this sum on squarefree inputs. [Tian, Theorem 4.7 and its normalization, pp. 57–58](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/iccm/2017/0005/0001/ICCM-2017-0005-0001-a003.pdf#page=7) · [Gross–Zagier, §I.7, Theorem 3](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.1007/BF01388809/fulltext.pdf#page=7) · [Coefficient and rank implications in Lean](../bounded/BoundedFromGenus.lean#L68)

*Proof.* Choose an integer $`m\ge\max(1,C)`$ once. Write $`\iota(n)`$ for the elliptic point that represents the integer $`n`$ in the polynomial construction.

**Multiplication.** Suppose the unguarded multiplication test accepts the codes of $`a,b,c`$ and $`f=ab-c\ne0`$. For each auxiliary slope $`u`$ in its certificate, the local congruences give $`M(u)^{25}\mid f`$. Thus $`h(u)\le m|f|^{24/25}`$. The finite-box lemma, already proved for every real coefficient at least one, gives $`\max(|a|,|b|,|c|)\lt (48m)^{13}`$. Set $`T=(48m)^{13}`$. The polynomial guard for this integer $`T`$ excludes every false triple in the box and accepts every true triple. The certificate for a true triple uses the rank input through pole completeness, and the proved representation by slopes. Both constructions precede the choice of $`T`$. This gives an exact Diophantine multiplication relation on the codes. [Local congruence and slope bound](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Multiplication/Soundness/ContactSupport.lean#L49) · [Finite-box lemma](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Multiplication/Soundness/FiniteBox.lean#L60) · [Guard for arbitrary $`T`$](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Multiplication/Certificate/Guard.lean#L37) · [True triples](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Multiplication/Completeness/AP/Completeness.lean#L62)

**Recovering the integer.** Put $`F(n)=\prod_{c\in\mathcal C}(n-c)`$. For $`n\notin\mathcal C`$, use the original local tests with shift $`\kappa`$ and the original precision program with shift $`m\kappa`$. The local tests imply $`\kappa\ne0`$ and $`M(t)\mid |\kappa F(n)|`$. Indeed the divisibility theorem uses the product outside the primes up to 324, and our $`S`$ contains those primes. If $`A_0\ge1`$ is the fixed integer coefficient of the precision program, then

```math
h(t)\le C|\kappa F(n)|^{24}\le A_0|m\kappa F(n)|^{24}.
```

The precision test gives $`|t-n|\lt \exp(-A_0|m\kappa F(n)|^{24})`$. A reduced $`t=a/b`$ distinct from $`n`$ has $`|t-n|\ge 1/b\ge\exp(-h(t))`$, so acceptance forces $`t=n`$. Conversely, first choose $`\kappa`$ by completeness of the local tests at $`t=n`$. The precision test at $`t=n`$ admits witnesses for **every** nonzero shift, hence for $`m\kappa`$. The witnesses for the two tests therefore coexist for the prescribed $`n`$. [Divisibility](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Assembly/Local/TestDivisibility.lean#L381) · [Existence of the local witnesses](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Assembly/Local/TestCompleteness.lean#L35) · [Precision at every nonzero shift](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Assembly/Close/Completeness.lean#L121)

Both tests are existential on the rational point codes. With the guard $`T`$, call them $`\mathrm{Test}_T`$ and $`\mathrm{Close}_T`$. The value test and the translation of the precision program use only the exact multiplication relation at this guard. Off the six constant cases the new formula is

```math
\exists\,N,K,K'\in\mathbb Q^5,\qquad
\mathrm{Test}_T(N,K,t)\ \land\ K'=[m]K\ \land\ \mathrm{Close}_T(N,K',t).
```

Here $`[m]`$ is multiplication by a fixed integer in the elliptic group. The two tests identify the integer represented by the common code $`N`$. Write $`V_T,D_m,L_T`$ for the polynomials expressing the value test, this scalar multiplication, and the precision test, respectively. Use disjoint tuples of auxiliary variables for the three polynomials. A single defining polynomial is then

```math
F(t)\bigl(V_T^2+D_m^2+L_T^2\bigr).
```

Over $`\mathbb Q`$ its vanishing gives precisely the six exceptional values or the conjunction of the three tests. Clear any fixed rational denominators. The Lean proof at $`T`$ uses the general translation theorem for the precision program and the composition law for the close relation. [Value test with arbitrary guard](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Assembly/Values/ValueTest.lean#L87) · [Precision translation](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Compiler/Translation/Rational.lean#L154) · [Close relation with arbitrary guard](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Assembly/Values/Outer.lean#L407) · [Fixed scalar multiplication](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q/blob/2358394558aafae202a1ce6d3bd1b536b79ad3d0/ZInQKernel/Syntax/Recipes/FixedScalar.lean#L181)

## The Picard sextic

Put $`s=5+120/t`$. The six marked points become $`\infty,-3,-1,0,1,3`$. In $`\mathbb Q(\rho)`$, with $`\rho^2=-3`$, set $`u=s+\rho`$, $`v=s-\rho`$, $`U=u^6`$ and $`V=v^6`$. For $`d\in\mathbb Q^\times`$ consider the smooth completion of

```math
2d\,y^2=f_{U,V}(x),\qquad
f_{U,V}(x)=(x^2+2x-2)\bigl(4U(2x-1)-Vx^3(x+4)\bigr).
```

The polynomial has leading coefficient $`-V`$ and discriminant $`-2^{22}3^6 U^2V^2(U-V)^6`$. Indeed, resultants are multiplicative and the resultant with the derivative gives the discriminant, including the sign in degree six. For rational $`s`$ the product $`UV=(s^2+3)^6`$ is nonzero, and $`U-V=12\rho s(s-1)(s+1)(s-3)(s+3)`$. Thus the noncontact fibres are smooth curves of genus two. Write $`J_s^{(d)}`$ for their Jacobians. [Discriminant of the sextic in Lean](../picard/PicardDiscriminant.lean) · [Independent Magma identity](../picard/PicardInvariant.m) · [Magma calculation of the discriminant and conjugation](../picard/SixContacts.m)

There are two useful identifications. First put $`A=U-V`$, $`a=2A^2`$ and $`w=-Av^2`$. Then $`w^3=-A^3V`$, $`a^2-4w^3=4A^3U`$ and the Picard curve $`Y^3=X^4+aX^2+w^3`$ has moduli coordinate $`j(s)=U/V`$. Substitution in Laga and Shnidman’s equation, followed by the change of ordinate $`y\mapsto vy`$, gives $`2y^2=f_{U,V}(x)`$. Their Remark 2.12 places this Jacobian in the isogeny class of the Prym. The quaternionic action on the Prym is therefore relevant to rational endomorphisms of the Jacobian. [Laga–Shnidman, Remark 2.12 and Theorem 6.5](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70347) · [Equation and moduli coordinate](../picard/PrymFactor.lean) · [Magma verification](../picard/PicardFamily.m)

Second, let $`k_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2},\sqrt{-3})`$ and choose $`b^2=-2`$ in $`k_0`$. The identity $`x^6f_{U,V}(-2/x)=-8f_{V,U}(x)`$ gives the change $`(x,y)\mapsto(-2/x,2by/x^3)`$, which exchanges $`U`$ and $`V`$. It extends across zero and infinity on the proper curves and induces an isomorphism of their unpointed Jacobians. Consequently, at every noncontact rational parameter and for every nonzero rational twist, the pullback of $`J_s^{(d)}`$ by any element of $`\operatorname{Gal}(k_0/\mathbb Q)`$ is isomorphic to $`J_s^{(d)}`$ as a group scheme over $`k_0`$. Constructing a rational factor also requires compatibility with endomorphisms. [Proper curves, Jacobians and Galois pullback](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardDescent.lean)

## A single twist and the local reduction test

Write $`s=A/B`$ in lowest terms with $`B\gt 0`$, and put

```math
N=AB(A-B)(A+B)(A-3B)(A+3B),\qquad
e_s=\prod_{\substack{p>3\\v_p(N)\equiv2\pmod4}}p .
```

At a noncontact parameter $`N\ne0`$. The positive squarefree integer $`e_s`$ chooses a single twist for all primes. To work integrally use $`u=A+\rho B`$, $`v=A-\rho B`$. This multiplies the rational sextic by the square $`B^6`$. The change of ordinate $`Y=2e_sB^3 y`$ gives the integral equation $`Y^2=2e_s f_{u^6,v^6}(x)`$. [Global twist and local coordinates](../picard/PrimitiveTwist.lean)

**Local calculation.** Fix $`p\gt 3`$ for which $`v_p(N)`$ is even, including zero. All six roots lie in a finite unramified extension of the coefficient completion. A cluster is the intersection of the roots with a closed disc. Apart from the full set and the singletons, the possibilities are as follows.

| Local coefficients | Clusters of size greater than one below the full set |
| --- | --- |
| $`u,v,u^6-v^6`$ are units | None |
| One of $`u,v`$ has order $`r\gt 0`$ | One triple of depth $`2r`$, using the reciprocal chart if $`v`$ is the nonunit |
| $`u,v`$ are units and $`v_p(u^6-v^6)=2r\gt 0`$ | Two triples of depth $`r`$ |

For a principal cluster $`S`$, Dokchitser, Dokchitser, Maistret and Morgan use $`\nu_S=v_p(c_f)+\sum_\alpha d_{S\wedge\{\alpha\}}`$, where $`c_f`$ is the leading coefficient of the integral equation and $`d`$ denotes cluster depth. In the one-triple case $`c_f`$ is a unit in the chosen chart, so the triple has $`\nu_S=6r`$ and the full set has $`\nu=0`$. In the two-triple case the full set is nonprincipal. Each triple has $`\nu_S=v_p(e_s)+3r`$, which is even by the choice of $`e_s`$. In the distinct-root case the full set has $`\nu=0`$. This accounts for the splitting field, all geometric clusters, and the parity of every required $`\nu_S`$. [Root valuations and principal clusters](../picard/LocalSupport.lean) · [DDMM, Theorem 1.9(5), restated as Theorem 10.3(5)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-021-02319-y)

The local and global Jacobians can be compared over the same field. For each such prime the root calculation supplies a finite unramified Witt ring, an embedding of the constant field into its fraction field, and the integral sextic displayed above. Over that fraction field the global twisted Jacobian is isomorphic to the Jacobian of the smooth completion of this very sextic. The formal application of DDMM identifies the root discs with the clusters of this equation, checks the odd cardinalities and principal parities above, and applies the general criterion for a split sextic. It transports the resulting abelian scheme along the Jacobian isomorphism. Its generic fibre is therefore the base change of the global twisted Jacobian. The criterion itself is an input from DDMM Theorem 10.3(5). [Local Jacobian and cluster configuration](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardLocalization.lean) · [Application of DDMM to the family](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardGoodReduction.lean)

The local calculation also accommodates the field of definition of the isogeny to the rational factor. Fix a finite normal extension $`M/\mathbb Q`$ containing the constants. Choose an integral primitive element with monic minimal polynomial $`P`$. Away from the primes dividing the nonzero integer $`N_M=\operatorname{Res}(P,P')`$, every residue root of $`P`$ is simple. Enlarge the finite residue field of the sextic until it contains such a root. Hensel’s lemma lifts that root to its Witt ring, giving an embedding of $`M`$ in the fraction field. Normality permits a choice of embedding that sends $`\rho`$ to the prescribed square root of $`-3`$. The six labelled roots, their depths and their principal parities persist in the enlarged ring. Equivalently, the existing abelian model has good base change to the larger Witt ring. [Compatible embedding and root labels](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/CommonWittField.lean) · [Base change of the good model](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/WittAscent.lean)

Products give good models for all positive powers. A surjective group homomorphism over $`M`$ from such a power to a rational abelian variety $`B_s`$ now localizes over the common field. Serre and Tate’s Corollary 2 passes good reduction to the quotient. Their Corollary 4 descends it to $`\mathbb Q_p`$. Thus outside $`\{2,3\}`$ and the prime divisors of $`N_M`$, even contact gives good reduction of the same $`B_s`$ used below in the height estimate. These additional primes depend only on $`M`$ and are absorbed by the uniform constant in Proposition 1. [Serre–Tate, Theorem 1, Corollaries 2 and 4](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Serre-Tate-Good_Reduction_of_Abelian_Varieties.pdf#page=3) · [Good reduction over a common field](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/CommonWittReduction.lean) · [Transfer from an unramified quotient](../picard/PicardAssembly.lean#L124) · [Products and local quotient maps](../picard/AbelianPowers.lean)

## Descent of a rational factor

Write $`A=J_s^{(e_s)}`$ for one smooth rational fibre. Over the algebraic closure, the non-CM fibres have the division quaternion algebra $`D`$ of discriminant six as their full endomorphism algebra. The curve is defined over $`\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})`$ and its moduli coordinate $`j(s)=((s+\rho)/(s-\rho))^6`$ has degree six. Since only finitely many CM values have degree at most two, only finitely many rational parameters are lost on restricting to the non-CM locus. Laga and Shnidman’s endomorphism-field formula places the Prym’s quaternionic action over $`L=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)`$. A rational quadratic twist preserves the endomorphism action because its sign is central. The descent application must transport the full algebra to the actual Jacobian over the same field. [Laga–Shnidman, Lemmas 6.2 and 6.16 and Corollary 6.6](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70347) · [Prym parameter and conjugate actions](../picard/PrymFactor.lean)

The finite exceptional set works for every nonzero rational twist. Over $`\overline{\mathbb Q}`$, the twisted Jacobian at a smooth parameter is isomorphic to the Jacobian of the canonical equation $`y^2=f_{j,1}(x)/2`$. The degree of the marked value $`j`$ is at most two and its rational parameter has at most six preimages per value. If $`N`$ marked values of degree at most two have a Jacobian containing an elliptic abelian subvariety, there is a single set of at most $`6N`$ rational parameters where this occurs. The comparison with the actual twist and the count are formalized. Finiteness of the marked values is the consequence of Laga–Shnidman used here. [Exceptional twisted fibres](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardCM.lean#L249)

For each automorphism $`\sigma`$ of $`L/\mathbb Q`$, the conjugate Jacobian is isomorphic to the original one. Suppose its full rational endomorphism algebra over $`L`$ is identified with $`D`$. A single unit intertwines the two embeddings on the quaternion generators, hence on all of $`D`$. Correcting the isomorphism by that unit gives an invertible rational homomorphism $`{}^{\sigma}\!A\to A`$ that commutes with all transported endomorphisms. This construction applies to each $`\sigma`$. [Quaternionic intertwining](../picard/PrymFactor.lean#L127) · [Compatible individual quasi-isogenies](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardConjugateEnd.lean#L175) · [Conjugate Jacobians](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/PicardDescent.lean)

To multiply these maps coherently one uses the group of semilinear automorphisms up to isogeny. For an abstract group extension whose conjugation on its kernel is inner and whose kernel has centre $`\mathbb Q^\times`$, one can choose a section $`s`$ centralizing the kernel, with $`s(1)=1`$. Its products satisfy $`s(g)s(h)=c(g,h)s(gh)`$ for a normalized rational two-cocycle $`c`$. Associativity gives the cocycle identity. For the actual surface the finite-quotient statement below assumes the classical multiplier construction on endomorphism-compatible isogenies. Its Lean formulation includes the canonical comparison between the pullback by a product and the two successive pullbacks. [Central section and normalized cocycle](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/CentralIsogenyCocycle.lean#L28)

**A common field for fibrewise descent.** Let $`G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)`$ for any finite Galois extension $`L`$. Tate’s theorem gives each rational-valued two-cocycle on $`G`$ an algebraic-valued primitive after inflation to the absolute Galois group. The coefficient group $`\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}`$ has the discrete topology and trivial action. The group $`H^2(G,\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times})`$ is finite. Choose one continuous primitive per class and take a finite Galois quotient through which they all factor. This proves that a single extension $`M/L`$, Galois over $`\mathbb Q`$, trivializes every rational-valued cocycle after extending its coefficients. [Ribet, Theorem 6.3 (Tate)](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/korea.pdf#page=13) · [Common finite quotient and finite second cohomology](../picard/UniformDescent.lean)

**Proposition 2 (Guitart applied to a fibre).** Suppose the preceding endomorphism and compatibility hypotheses hold for $`A`$. Let $`\beta`$ be a splitting cochain for the normalized cocycle on $`H=\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)`$, and let $`E=\mathbb Q(\beta(h):h\in H)`$. Put $`m=[E:\mathbb Q]`$. Guitart’s Proposition 2.10 gives an abelian variety $`B_s/\mathbb Q`$ and an isogeny $`\kappa:A^m\to B_s`$ over the algebraic closure. Its formula is

```math
\kappa^{-1}{}^{\sigma}\kappa=\phi(\beta(\sigma))^{-1}\widehat\mu_{\sigma}.
```

Here $`\phi`$ is the regular representation of $`E`$ over $`\mathbb Q`$, and $`\widehat\mu_\sigma`$ is the diagonal action of the compatible conjugate isogeny on the power. Choose the identity as the conjugate isogeny at the neutral element of $`G`$. For $`\sigma`$ fixing $`M`$, both terms on the right are then the identity. Thus $`\kappa`$ is defined over $`M`$. The formal proof passes from equality in rational homomorphisms to equality of morphisms using surjectivity of multiplication by a nonzero integer. Galois descent gives a unique morphism over $`M`$, and finiteness and surjectivity descend with it. [Descent of the isogeny from the splitting law](../picard/GuitartDescent.lean)

By Corollary 2.12, $`\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}^0(B_s)\simeq E\otimes_{\mathbb Q}D`$. A quadratic field in this algebra has degree $`2m=\dim B_s`$ over $`\mathbb Q`$, as required for von Känel’s theorem. [Guitart, Proposition 2.10 and Corollary 2.12](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/38384#page=6) · [Application to the Picard family](../picard/fibrewise-descent.md)

Choose quaternion generators $`i^2=-3`$, $`j^2=2`$ and $`ji=-ij`$. If $`-3`$ is a nonsquare in $`E`$, the element $`i`$ already generates a quadratic field. Otherwise put $`r^2=-3`$ in $`E`$. For $`b,c\in E`$ one has

```math
(bj+cij)^2=2(b+rc)(b-rc).
```

This represents every scalar in $`E`$. Indeed the two factors $`b+rc`$ and $`b-rc`$ can be assigned independently. Some $`d\in E`$ is a nonsquare, since a number field in which every element is a square would contain roots of unity of order $`2^n`$ for every $`n`$. An element whose square is $`d`$ gives the required embedding of $`E[T]/(T^2-d)`$. [A quadratic field for every coefficient field](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/GeneralQuaternionField.lean)

The equation defining $`\beta`$ shows that the rational span of its values is closed under multiplication. It contains one and is a finite-dimensional domain, hence equals $`E`$. Thus $`m\leq |H|=[M:\mathbb Q]`$. This bounds $`\dim B_s=2m`$ independently of $`s`$. For good reduction one may use $`\kappa`$ itself. For the height estimate, apply Gaudron–Rémond over $`M`$ with source $`B_s`$. This gives an isogeny in the reverse direction, from $`B_s`$ to $`A^m`$, of degree at most $`cH_s^k`$, where $`H_s=\max(1,h_F(B_s))`$. The constants are uniform because the base field is fixed and the dimensions range over a finite set. [Coefficient field spanned by the cochain](../picard/UniformDescent.lean) · [Gaudron–Rémond, Théorème 1.4](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~gaudron/art13.pdf#page=4) · [von Känel, Theorem 6.2 and (6.7)](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2021.29)

Additivity of the stable height on products and its change under an isogeny give $`m h_F(A)\leq h_F(B_s)+(\log c+k\log H_s)/2`$. Since $`m\ge1`$ and $`H_s\ge1`$, it follows that

```math
\max(1,h_F(A))\leq(1+k/2)H_s+\tfrac12\max(0,\log c).
```

Thus a linear upper bound for the moduli height in terms of the stable height of the Jacobian transfers to the rational factor. The degree of the original descent isogeny need not be estimated. [Isogeny degree and stable height](../Lean/Lemmas/picard-application/IsogenyHeight.lean)

## From a theta value to a height bound

Write $`J=J(t)`$ for Glazer’s degree-twelve rational coordinate, and $`Q_2`$ for the quadratic transvectant in the ordinary coefficient convention. The weight-zero invariant of the sextic is

```math
R=\frac{Q_2(f)^5}{\operatorname{disc}(f)}
  =\frac{(J+4)^5}{200000J^3}.
```

If $`J=a/b\ne0`$ and $`R=P/Q`$ are reduced fractions with positive denominators, then $`a/b`$ is a root of $`Q(X+4)^5-200000PX^3`$. Hence $`b\mid Q`$ and $`a\mid1024Q`$. This gives $`h(J)\le h(R)+\log1024`$. The height inequality for Glazer’s coordinate yields $`12h(t)\le h(R)+450+\log1024`$ away from the contacts. [Actual discriminant and rational invariant](../picard/PicardDiscriminant.lean) · [Rational-root estimate](../bounded/ModuliHeight.lean)

To express $`R`$ in period coordinates, let $`x`$ be a representative of the sixteen level-two theta nulls for the canonical principal polarization. For one symplectic homology basis, Pazuki identifies this projective vector with the null values at the sixteen half-characteristics. Its projective coordinate ratios are algebraic and its Fubini–Study height is his theta height. [Pazuki, Definition 2.6 and the following analytic description](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMF_2012__140_1_19_0.pdf#page=13)

There are ten even characteristics. In Streng’s notation $`h_{10}`$ is the product of the squares of their ten coordinates. The form $`h_{12}`$ sums over the fifteen Göpel quadruples, taking for each quadruple the product of the fourth powers of its six complementary coordinates. The sum over the fifteen pairings of the six roots gives Streng’s $`I_2=-120Q_2`$. With a common weighted scale for $`I_2,I_{10}`$ and any projective scale for $`x`$, the two scales cancel and give

```math
R=\frac{h_{12}(x)^5}{(-120)^5h_{10}(x)^6},
\qquad h(R)\le120h_\Theta(x).
```

Both polynomials in the fraction are homogeneous of degree 120. At a finite place the ultrametric inequality bounds them in terms of the largest coordinate. At an infinite place the Euclidean norm absorbs both the fifteen summands and the factor 120. The product formula gives the displayed bound for algebraic theta coordinates over any number field. The discriminant identity ensures $`h_{10}\ne0`$ once the period formulas hold. The bound also applies when $`I_2=0`$. [Streng, published Lemma 7.3 and Remark 7.10](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2013-02712-3) · [Normalization and height in Lean](../picard/ThetaComparison.lean)

### Direct degree bounds

One can instead start with a family of degree bounds. Write $`H_s=h_F(B_s)`$. It suffices to have $`1\leq\dim B_s\leq G`$ and positive isogeny degrees $`D_s\leq c\max(1,H_s)^k`$ for fixed $`G,k\in\mathbb N`$ and $`c\gt 0`$. Indeed, $`\log D_s\leq\log c+k\log\max(1,H_s)`$, so the coefficient 84 in the bound for $`h(R)`$ can be replaced by $`84+42k`$, with additive constant $`289200+42\log c`$. ### An explicit field giving dimension at most sixteen

For $`L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{24})=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)`$ all these classes can be split over the explicit field

```math
M=L\bigl(\sqrt[4]{2},\sqrt[4]{3},\sqrt{1+\sqrt{-2}}\bigr).
```

Three independent square classes over $`L`$ give $`[M:\mathbb Q]=64`$. The defining quartics split in $`M`$ and their discriminants have prime factors only 2 and 3. Both primes already ramify in $`L`$. The commutators of lifts of the three sign changes on $`L`$ have rank three on the three square roots. The three resulting forms span the space of alternating bicharacters of $`G\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)^3`$. After their contributions are removed, a symmetric cocycle splits by rescaling commuting lifts with algebraic square roots. These field and commutator calculations were carried out in Magma. [Descent argument and its geometric hypotheses](../picard/fibrewise-descent.md) · [Magma field and commutator computation](../picard/FibrewiseDescent.m)

For this field the kernel of $`H\to G`$ has exponent two. On the kernel $`\beta`$ takes only the values $`\pm1`$, so the eight values at coset representatives span $`E`$. Hence $`m\leq8`$ and $`\dim B_s\leq16`$. [Coefficient degree from the cosets](../picard/FibrewiseProjector.lean#L146)

### An alternative using a reference fibre

One can also lift a rational projector in a family. On an individual fibre, the twisted regular representation has a rational idempotent commuting with its left shifts whose image is isomorphic to $`E`$. The following specialization lemma explains how to extend a geometric splitting from one parameter. [Equivariant projector](../picard/FibrewiseProjector.lean#L63)

**Lemma 3 (specialization on a Weil restriction).** Let $`U`$ be a smooth geometrically integral curve over a field $`k`$ of characteristic zero, $`L/k`$ finite Galois, $`s_0\in U(k)`$, and $`J`$ a projective abelian scheme over $`U_L`$. Suppose every Galois conjugate of $`J`$ is isogenous to $`J`$ over $`U_L`$, and every element of $`\operatorname{End}^0_L(J_{s_0})`$ lifts to $`\operatorname{End}^0_{U_L}(J)`$. Write $`\operatorname{End}^0=\operatorname{End}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q`$. For $`W=\operatorname{Res}_{U_L/U}J`$, evaluation at $`s_0`$ is an isomorphism of rational algebras

```math
\operatorname{End}^0_U(W)\longrightarrow \operatorname{End}^0_k(W_{s_0}).
```

*Proof.* A homomorphism between abelian schemes that vanishes on one fibre vanishes on the connected base. By rigidity it factors through the base, and its value on the zero section is zero. Clearing denominators gives injectivity for rational homomorphisms. Over $`U_L`$, the Weil restriction is the product of the conjugates of $`J`$. Choose isogenies from these conjugates to $`J`$ and invert them in the category up to isogeny. A homomorphism between two factors on the chosen fibre is now an endomorphism of $`J_{s_0}`$. Lift that endomorphism and compose with the chosen isogenies and their inverses. This lifts each matrix entry, proving surjectivity over $`U_L`$. Evaluation is equivariant because $`s_0`$ is $`k`$-rational. Taking Galois invariants gives the asserted isomorphism by descent for morphisms. In particular a rational projector and a field acting on its image lift together. [Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, Proposition 20.1](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/AVs.pdf#page=42) · [Descent for morphisms](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/023Q)

**Splitting the projector.** The same argument lifts rectangular matrices of homomorphisms. Suppose a projector $`p`$ on $`W`$ has been lifted, and over $`L`$ its image at $`s_0`$ is isomorphic up to isogeny to $`J_{s_0}^r`$. Lift the two splitting maps $`i_0:J_{s_0}^r\to W_{s_0}`$ and $`q_0:W_{s_0}\to J_{s_0}^r`$ entry by entry. Injectivity of specialization gives $`qi=1`$ and $`iq=p`$ from the two identities at $`s_0`$. Hence every splitting of $`p`$ has image isomorphic to $`J^r`$ in the isogeny category. [Rectangular homomorphisms and splitting](../picard/PrymFactor.lean) · [Invariant projector and coefficient field](../bounded/EndomorphismSpecialization.lean)

For this construction one must lift the full endomorphism algebra of the reference fibre and extend a representative isogeny with finite flat kernel over a common open. Its degree is then fixed throughout that open. Simultaneous quadratic twisting preserves the map because the sign acts centrally. [Spreading out, Milne, Remark 20.9](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/AVs.pdf#page=44)
